# Discovery Dates — Curated Historical Dates for Dutch Newspaper Research

Use when suggesting 3 dates in Discovery mode. Select dates that span different eras
and themes. Never suggest 3 dates from the same decade.

When presenting to user, format as:
  [N]. [Date] — [Newspaper] — [Teaser]

---

## Early Republic & Golden Age (1618–1795)

- **15 November 1618** — Oprechte Haerlemsche Courant
  Synod of Dort in session; Dutch Reformed Church defining orthodoxy while merchants trade globally.

- **12 January 1672** — Amsterdamsche Courant
  Rampjaar begins: France, England, Münster and Cologne declare war simultaneously. The Republic nearly collapses.

- **30 October 1688** — s'Gravenhaegse Courant
  William III sails for England with 500 ships; the Glorious Revolution reported from Dutch perspective.

- **5 February 1795** — Leydsche Courant
  Batavian Republic proclaimed; patriots celebrate as French troops enter. Old regent oligarchy gone overnight.

---

## Napoleonic & Early Kingdom (1795–1850)

- **18 June 1815** — Nederlandsche Staatscourant
  Battle of Waterloo. Netherlands' new king William I has everything at stake just north of the battlefield.

- **4 October 1830** — Algemeene Konst- en Letter-Bode
  Belgian Revolution; Netherlands loses its southern provinces after just 15 years of union.

- **17 March 1848** — Nieuwe Rotterdamsche Courant
  Revolutions across Europe; Thorbecke drafts new Dutch constitution — Netherlands avoids upheaval.

---

## Industrial Age & Colonial Expansion (1850–1900)

- **17 November 1863** — Algemeen Handelsblad
  Slavery abolished in Surinam and Dutch Antilles — 10 years after Denmark, 30 years after Britain.

- **10 October 1872** — Het Nieuws van den Dag
  First Dutch railway strikes; industrialization meets organized labor for the first time.

- **14 June 1881** — De Telegraaf (proto-edition) / Het Vaderland
  Diamond drilling begins in South Africa; Dutch settlers (Boers) and British capital on collision course.

- **31 August 1898** — De Courant
  Queen Wilhelmina crowned at 18; celebrating 100 years of the House of Orange in a newly industrialized Netherlands.

---

## Belle Époque & Pre-WWI Tension (1900–1914)

- **29 December 1903** — Nieuwe Rotterdamsche Courant
  Massive railway strike crushed by emergency law; Troelstra's socialists outmaneuvered. Labor rights set back a decade.

- **28 June 1914** — Algemeen Handelsblad
  Archduke Franz Ferdinand assassinated in Sarajevo. Dutch papers react to news that will end their world.

- **4 August 1914** — De Tijd / Het Vaderland
  Netherlands declares neutrality as WWI erupts. Mobilization of 200,000 men; fear of German invasion.

---

## WWI Neutrality & Interwar Crisis (1914–1940)

- **11 November 1918** — De Telegraaf
  WWI armistice. Neutral Netherlands survived but is exhausted, blockaded, and flooded with refugees.

- **13 November 1918** — Het Volk
  Troelstra's socialist revolution attempt fails spectacularly — within 48 hours of trying to overthrow the monarchy.

- **29 October 1929** — Nieuwe Rotterdamsche Courant
  Wall Street crash. Dutch papers struggle to explain what just happened to global capitalism.

- **30 January 1933** — De Telegraaf
  Hitler appointed Chancellor. Dutch papers divided: threat or German domestic matter?

---

## WWII Occupation (1940–1945)

- **15 May 1940** — Algemeen Handelsblad (last free edition)
  Day after Dutch capitulation. Final free newspapers before German censorship begins.

- **6 June 1944** — Illegale pers / clandestine editions
  D-Day. Underground newspapers report Allied landings; occupation press forbidden to mention it.

- **5 May 1945** — De Vrije Pers / Liberation papers
  Liberation Day for the western Netherlands. First free newspapers in 5 years — chaotic, jubilant, disbelieving.

---

## Reconstruction & Cold War (1945–1975)

- **1 February 1953** — De Telegraaf / NRC
  Day after the North Sea Flood (Watersnoodramp). 1,836 dead; Zeeland and South Holland submerged.
  Delpher has remarkable coverage: rescue operations, village-by-village death tolls.

- **27 December 1949** — Nieuwe Rotterdamsche Courant
  Dutch sovereignty over Indonesia transferred. End of 350 years of colonial empire, reported with denial and grief.

- **20 July 1969** — De Telegraaf
  Moon landing. Dutch reaction to Americans walking on the moon — Cold War framing, national pride absent.

---

## Selection Tips for Discovery Mode

When choosing 3 dates to suggest:
- Pick one pre-1900, one 1900–1945, one post-1945
- Match era to user's research context if "My Research Themes" note is loaded
  (e.g., user researching WWII → offer 1940, 1944, 1945 dates)
- Prioritize dates with high Delpher coverage (post-1870 generally better OCR)
- Avoid suggesting the same era as a page the user just analyzed
